Research

Deciding when you don't really know your odds

The common thread: many economic decisions are made without known probabilities. I try to measure what people believe, how they react to that uncertainty, and what it means for public policy.

PhD thesis · since 2026

Essays on the role of beliefs and ambiguity: migration and the labour market

Moving abroad for work means deciding without knowing your real chances: the wage, the job, whether your degree will be recognised. Standard migration models assume known risks. My thesis starts from the opposite idea: the probabilities themselves are uncertain, and the way people react to this ambiguity can change who moves, where, and with what results.

  1. 01 Theory

    Who moves when the odds are unknown?

    Roy and Borjas's model of migrant self-selection, revisited with the smooth ambiguity model of Klibanoff, Marinacci and Mukerji and a Nishimura–Ozaki reservation wage.

  2. 02 Experiment

    How do people judge their chances of finding a job?

    An experiment that measures what people believe about their chances of labour market integration, and their attitude towards the ambiguity around those chances.

  3. 03 Experiment

    Can information reduce ambiguity?

    People do not react to uncertainty in the same way depending on where it comes from. A randomised information intervention tests whether better information reduces ambiguity and changes choices.

Starting points: Roy (1951), Sjaastad (1962), Todaro (1969), Harris and Todaro (1970), Borjas (1987), Manski (2004), Nishimura and Ozaki (2004), Klibanoff, Marinacci and Mukerji (2005), Abdellaoui et al. (2011).

Master's thesis · funded by UPPA

The tragedy of the commons: beliefs, ambiguity, social preferences and public policy

Why do people who live and work by the beach in Saint-Louis, Senegal, chip in to keep it clean, or not? Some hold back because they count on the others, some because they expect nothing from them, and the usual protocols cannot tell the two apart.

I build a protocol that measures what each person believes about the others' contributions, and how much weight they give to these beliefs when they are uncertain (source method, Prelec weighting function). I first test it blind on 2,500 simulated residents: the estimation recovers their parameters without systematic bias.

  • Time series · Machine learning

    Is artificial intelligence driving speculative bubbles?

    Microsoft, Tesla and the Nasdaq index from 2021 to 2026. GSADF and BSADF tests detect explosive behaviour for Tesla, with two bubble episodes (2021-2022 and early 2023), while Microsoft and the Nasdaq show no lasting bubble. Empirical mode decomposition and wavelets show that volatility comes mostly from the short term. A random forest fed with Google Trends and Wikipedia data completes the analysis.

    2.965 GSADF statistic for Tesla, against a threshold of 1.894

  • Spatial econometrics

    France's 2021 energy rating reform: an effect that spills across départements

    Housing energy performance is not spread at random across French départements. Moran's I and Lagrange multiplier tests reveal spatial dependence that ordinary least squares miss. A spatial error model built on a contiguity matrix shows that shocks hitting one département partly spill over to its neighbours.

    λ = 0.424 spatial error coefficient, significant at 1%

  • Economic geography

    Remote work: dispersion or agglomeration?

    With Taoufiq Bouyarmane. We analyse the calibrated spatial model of Delventhal and Parkhomenko (2020) and its counterfactual simulations. Remote work durably reshapes where households live and where jobs are, without spelling the end of cities, with consequences for transport, local taxation and housing.

    +47% home–work distance, while total commuting time falls by 25%

  • International trade · Gravity model

    Does a shared currency boost trade in West Africa?

    A bilateral panel of ECOWAS countries from 1995 to 2025, built from UN Comtrade, World Bank and CEPII data and estimated by PPML with exporter, importer and year fixed effects. Economic size and distance play the roles the gravity model predicts, and infrastructure and political stability matter too. Belonging to the WAEMU monetary union does not raise trade on its own, which the report links to non-tariff barriers and similar, commodity-based exports.

    −0.073 coefficient on joint WAEMU membership, significant at 1%

  • Survey · Data analysis

    Do banks meet the needs of students in Pau?

    A questionnaire I designed and analysed with Sphinx IQ3 and R, answered by 52 students. Most are well banked: cards and mobile apps are part of daily life and about half save regularly. Gaps remain, though: paperwork is the main hurdle to opening an account, half would like a student loan, and many want practical help with managing their money.

    65% expect help with managing money from local bodies such as the Crous
